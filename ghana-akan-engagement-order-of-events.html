Ghanaian (Akan) engagement: the order of events

A Ghanaian engagement, often called the knocking or the traditional marriage, follows a pattern led by the Okyeame (spokespersons). Here is how the day usually goes for an Akan family. Other Ghanaian groups differ.

Order of events

  1. Arrival and seating of both families
  2. Opening prayer
  3. Introductions by each family's spokesperson (Okyeame)
  4. Greetings and welcome of guests and witnesses
  5. Knocking (Kokooko), if not done earlier
  6. Purpose of the visit is stated
  7. The groom is called in and presented
  8. Request to see the bride (and the fake brides!)
  9. The bride enters
  10. Presentation and checking of the engagement list
  11. The bride is asked three times, and she accepts
  12. The head drink (Tiri nsa) is accepted
  13. Presentation of the ring and Bible
  14. Prayers, blessings and advice from the elders
  15. Thanksgiving (Aseda) and vote of thanks
  16. Couple's first dance, reception and photos

What families usually bring

  • Engagement Bible
  • Engagement ring
  • Wax-print cloth (6 pieces)
  • Kente (optional)
  • Jewellery set
  • Shoes and sandals
  • Handbags
  • Underwear and nightwear
  • Toiletries and perfume
  • Wristwatch
  • Suitcase
  • Head drink (Tiri nsa)
  • Bride price (as agreed)
  • For her father: cloth, drink and money
  • For her mother: cloth, headtie and money
  • Money for her brothers (Akontasekan)
  • Thanksgiving drinks (Aseda)
  • Cooking utensils / start-up money

Every family's list is different, and some towns add or drop items. Use this as a starting point and check it with your elders.
