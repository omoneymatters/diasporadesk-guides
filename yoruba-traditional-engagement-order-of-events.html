Yoruba traditional engagement: the order of events

A Yoruba engagement (the introduction) is run by the two Alágas, and the day follows a fairly steady pattern. This is the order most families recognise. Yours may shuffle a step or two.

Order of events

  1. Arrival and seating of guests
  2. Opening prayer
  3. Groom's family at the gate (owó ìkanlẹ̀kùn)
  4. Families greet and are introduced by the Alágas
  5. Reading of the proposal letter
  6. Reading of the acceptance letter
  7. Groom and his friends prostrate (Ìdọ̀bálẹ̀)
  8. The groom is seated
  9. Presentation of Ẹrù Ìdána and owó orí
  10. Entrance of the bride with her friends
  11. Bride kneels for both sets of parents
  12. Bride finds the groom and puts on his fila
  13. Bride picks the Bible or Quran; prayers over the items
  14. Exchange of rings
  15. Tasting of obì, orógbó, ataare, honey and salt; elders pray
  16. Cake, first dance, vote of thanks and reception

What families usually bring

  • Bible or Quran
  • Engagement ring
  • Umbrella
  • Suitcase (aṣọ òkè, gèlè, jewellery, shoes)
  • Kola nuts (Obì àbàtà)
  • Bitter kola (Orógbó)
  • Alligator pepper (Ataare)
  • Honey (Oyin)
  • Salt (Iyọ̀)
  • Sugar
  • Yams (Iṣu)
  • Palm oil (Epo pupa)
  • Fruit basket
  • Drinks
  • Envelopes for the families
  • Owó orí (bride price)
  • Aadùn
  • Coconuts
  • Bag of rice
  • Wristwatch and handbag

Lists differ by family, so use this as a starting point and check it with your elders.
